Arachnids can be a surprising starting point for conversations about biodiversity, behavior, and evolution. Through museum events, school activities, art, and public talks, I share how scientists study these animals and invite people to look more closely at organisms they may otherwise overlook.

## Museums and public events

### Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History

I participate in public science events at the Smithsonian in Washington, DC, including activities in Q?rius. I enjoy speaking with visitors about pelican spiders, field research in Madagascar, and the observations that lead to scientific questions.

### Science events in Argentina

I have taken part in *La Noche de los Museos* in Córdoba and other museum-based activities, introducing visitors to arachnid diversity and research. Earlier in my career, I also participated in *Eight-Legged Encounters* at the Bohart Museum of Entomology in California.

## Science with schools

With support from a Society for the Study of Evolution outreach grant, I helped develop activities about arthropod diversity and evolution with rural and urban schools in central Argentina. These experiences brought students into contact with local organisms and created opportunities to discuss how we observe, compare, and investigate biodiversity.

## Art and scientific observation

In Argentina, I organized the collective art-and-science exhibition *Miradas de un mundo pequeño* and a workshop on scientific illustration in entomology. Drawing and photography can encourage the same close attention to detail that is essential in biological research, while offering different ways to engage with the natural world.

## Building community in arachnology

I am a founding member of the **Red de Aracnología Emergente Latina (RAEL)**, a network that promotes collaboration, communication, and opportunities for early-career arachnologists across Latin America. I have also contributed to activities that share arachnological research and make the people behind it more visible.

## Talks, conversations, and media

I have given public talks about arachnids, sexual selection, and my field research in Madagascar. My work and science communication activities have also included podcast conversations, online educational events, and interviews with science media.

These formats allow me to share both research findings and the process behind them: the field observations, questions, experiments, and collaborations that shape a scientific project.
