I enjoy helping students turn curiosity about organisms into questions they can investigate. My teaching and mentoring connect natural history, field and laboratory methods, quantitative analysis, and scientific communication. I aim to make the reasoning behind each step visible: how we develop a question, choose a method, interpret a result, and revise an explanation.

## Teaching

### Entomology

**Teaching Assistant · Universidad Nacional de Córdoba, Argentina · 2018–2024**

I supported undergraduate teaching in entomology, helping students study arthropod diversity, morphology, identification, and natural history. I particularly value hands-on learning with specimens: careful observation often leads to the most interesting questions about ecology and evolution.

## Research mentoring

### Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History

**Research internship co-mentor · 2026**

I co-mentored a student in the Natural History Research Experiences program. The project involved developing a research question, collecting data from museum specimens, using geometric morphometrics, analyzing results, and interpreting biological patterns.

### Field research in Madagascar

**Field-team training and supervision · 2025**

During two field seasons, I trained Malagasy students and early-career researchers in arachnid sampling, ecological and behavioral protocols, care of live animals, and specimen preservation. Working together in the field also meant adapting methods to local conditions, keeping reliable records, and discussing how observations could become testable questions.

### Undergraduate research in Argentina

**Thesis supervision and research-assistant training · 2023–2024**

I supervised or co-supervised undergraduate thesis projects at the Universidad Nacional de Córdoba and trained research assistants in field collection, animal care, laboratory experiments, and research workflows. I supported students as they developed increasing independence in collecting, organizing, and interpreting their data.

### International mentoring

**Animal Behaviour Collective · 2024**

I participated as a mentor in an international mentoring program, supporting an early-career student in navigating research questions and academic development.

## My approach

I start by learning what a student already knows and what they hope to learn. We then define a manageable question, agree on clear milestones, and build skills through practice and feedback. As the project develops, I encourage students to take ownership of decisions while knowing they can ask questions, reconsider an approach, and learn from unexpected results.

I am particularly interested in mentoring projects that combine field observations, behavioral data, museum specimens, imaging, or quantitative analyses. These approaches offer students different ways to contribute to a shared scientific question while developing skills they can carry into their own work.
